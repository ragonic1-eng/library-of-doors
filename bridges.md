# Bridges: how the books of The Library of Doors link up

The owner's rule (2026-10-24): every book is linked to the ones before it, in a way that fits the author. This page
holds what the authors have in common, the method every bridge follows, the Sandman → Neverwhere bridge as built,
and the plan for the next two doors. It supersedes the bridge parts of `series-bible.md` §2b and §6.

---

## 1. The author: what Neil Gaiman's books have in common

Three of the four books are Gaiman's (*The Sandman*, *Neverwhere*, *Coraline*). Read side by side, the same ideas
come back, and a bridge built from them feels like his writing rather than a crossover:

| Thread | In The Sandman | In Neverwhere | In Coraline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A world right beside ours, reached through a door** | the Dreaming, entered every night | London Below, through a door in a brick wall | the Other flat, through a bricked-up door |
| **The forgotten and the overlooked** | dreams forgotten on waking; gods no one believes in | people who "fall through the cracks"; the homeless no one sees | the lost children in the mirror |
| **Kindness given comes back** | Aurie's mercy decides who stands with her at the end | Richard stops for a bleeding stranger; the novel opens with the *Lyke Wake Dirge*: whoever gave hosen and shoon is protected on the Bridge of Dread | the ghosts and the cat help the girl who helped them |
| **Death is kind, and endings belong to stories** | Death, Aurie's sister | Hunter's death, the marquis's death and return | the beldam's end down the well |
| **Old myth played straight in modern clothes, with dry humour** | Lucifer in a piano bar; Shakespeare's bargain | an earl's court in a Tube carriage; an angel under Islington | a fairy-tale witch in a flat |
| **Bargains, prices, favours owed** | Burgess, Lucifer's key, Orphea's price | the marquis's favours, Lamia's payment, Islington's offer | the button eyes |
| **Dark doubles** | the Corinthian, the false house | the "other Richard" in the Ordeal | the Other Mother |

Gaiman on his own work: he has said he believes "in stories" and "in people", and that he loves doors because they
are "anything that leads to possibilities"; *Neverwhere* began as his BBC series with Lenny Henry (1996), his first
big work after *The Sandman*, and reviewers called it the comic's dreaming landscape moved under London.
Sources: [neilgaiman.com: Neverwhere](https://neilgaiman.com/works/Television/Neverwhere/),
[Wikipedia: Neverwhere](https://Wikipedia.com/wiki/Neverwhere),
[Rambles review (Sandman fingerprints)](https://www.rambles.net/gaiman_never97.html),
[Hachette: Neverwhere ("I love doors")](https://hachette.co.uk/titles/neil-gaiman-2/neverwhere/9780755322800),
[Stanford Daily, 2018 (stories, people, dark doubles)](https://stanforddaily.com/2018/11/16/the-stardust-surrounding-neil-gaiman/),
[Kirkus review](https://kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/neil-gaiman/neverwhere-gaiman); the novel itself,
`source-text/neverwhere.txt` (epigraphs, the Beast dream in chapter 2).

**The fourth author is different.** *The Alchemist* is Paulo Coelho's: a fable, not a Gaiman world. Its threads
are the Personal Legend, omens, the Soul of the World, and a recurring dream that starts the journey
([LitCharts: The Alchemist](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-alchemist/themes/the-pursuit-of-your-personal-legend)).
So its bridge goes through the one thing both writers share, the **dream**, and through the library frame, and
never brings Gaiman's characters into Coelho's desert (§5).

## 2. The method: every bridge has the same six parts

Aurie is always Dream underneath. She dreams herself into each book's hero and wakes in Lucienne's library at the
end (`series-bible.md` §2b). Every bridge is built from these parts, in this order:

1. **The reason to go.** Not "another door": a story reason that comes out of the previous book. A thing from
   the earlier book has leaked into the next one, or the next one needs exactly what she became.
2. **One opening per earlier ending.** The new book's first scene reads how the previous book ended and opens
   differently for each ending, plus one opening for a player who has not finished it. The new book never
   contradicts how she left the last one.
3. **One thread from the author's own text** that runs through both books (a song, an object, a dream), planted in
   the opening and paid off inside the new book.
4. **The guardian at the dead ends: Death.** Because Aurie is dreaming, she cannot die in a book: at every dead end
   her sister meets her and sends her back to choose again. One shared clip per book, the same everywhere, which
   makes the dead ends part of the story instead of a game-over screen.
5. **Echoes: three to six small callbacks** inside the new book to particular things she did before (a flag, a scene
   reached). Words, not pictures, except at most one still. At least one should be an extra *choice*, not only a line.
6. **The way out.** Waking in the library with the relic; Lucienne names something she did there; the book is
   shelved beside the others.

Rules: bridges cost words, not money (each bridge so far: a handful of stills and one shared clip). Only characters
whose sheets were already approved appear in a bridge (no new sheets just for a link). Anything not in the source is
marked ◆ in the story map. Every reference to another book is checked on every build (§4).

## 3. The Sandman → Neverwhere bridge, as built

**The reason to go.** Lucienne's report: a dream has come into the library from the waking world that belongs to no
one there: a sewer, a boar the size of an ox, a spear in a hand that is not quite a hand. It is Richard Mayhew's
dream from the novel's second chapter, the Beast of London. Someone in London dreams it every night and never reaches
the end, and a dream nobody finishes becomes a door ◆. To walk it from the inside, the Queen of Dreams becomes its
dreamer: one woman, one life, an office off the Strand, a name: **Aurie Mayhew**. The same dream then plays again in
Neverwhere's scene 2.1, and the player recognises it; it comes true at the Beast (19.1), and if she throws the spear
"as in the dream", it kills her (19.2).

**One opening per Sandman ending** (scene **0.1 The library between**, the same title as the Sandman's library scene):

| How the Sandman ended | How Neverwhere opens |
|---|---|
| Not finished yet | The Dreaming is still mending, but the library stands, and one of the three new doors has begun to rattle, as if someone were knocking from the other side. |
| **The Wake** (end-A: Aurie died, Daniel is Dream) | At her wake they sang the oldest song for the dead, the *Lyke Wake Dirge*. The Dreaming kept her, as it keeps every dream it is not ready to lose. Daniel finds her among the shelves: you always wanted to know what it is like to be one of them. One life. No idea how it ends. |
| **The King Who Would Not Change** (end-B: a realm of ash) | The nightmares she can no longer hold drift into the waking world; one has found a sleeper in London. It is her nightmare, and she goes in to fetch it back. |
| **The Deserter** (end-C: she walked away) | She already lives in London: a flat over a bakery, an office off the Strand, a fiancée called Jessica, a name nobody questions. She does not have to become anyone; she only has to remember to open the door. |
| **The Song That Never Ends** (end-R: she keeps everything) | Lucienne brings her the only new thing in the Dreaming, a door that was not there yesterday. A queen who will never change can still read. |

If she has finished the Sandman more than once, the most recent ending counts.

**The thread: the Lyke Wake Dirge.** It is Neverwhere's own epigraph (Gaiman put it there): a wake song about crossing
the Bridge of Dread, protected by the kindness you showed in life. It is sung at Aurie's wake (0.1), she hears a
verse on Night's Bridge, the novel's Bridge of Dread, and remembers it if she died at the Wake (6.2), and it runs in
her head through the labyrinth after the Beast ("fire and fleet and candlelight", 19.5). It carries the Sandman's
question, mercy, into Neverwhere's, loyalty.

**Death at every dead end.** Lucienne plants it at the door (0.2): if it goes wrong in there, you will only wake;
your sister will see to that. At each of the nine dead ends, Death is waiting: you don't die in a dream, little
sister. You wake. Go back, and choose better. If Aurie died at the Wake: you did this properly once. This isn't that.

**Echoes** (each read from her Sandman save):

| Neverwhere scene | If in the Sandman she... | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 2.2 Two gentlemen at the door | cursed Alex to wake for ever | Vandemar sniffs: we heard of a man who dreams of waking. Nasty. |
| 4.1 The Monday nobody sees | made Hob Gadling her friend | the only person in London who can see her is a man outside the White Horse, drinking there since 1389: I know you. From somewhere. (one still) |
| 6.2 Night's Bridge | died at the Wake | she remembers the dirge was sung for her |
| 20.4 The angel's hall | met Lucifer (Sandman 2.6) | a new choice: "I've sat across a table from Lucifer. I've met better angels than you." It goads the killers like the canon line, and opens the same rescue |
| 21.2 Nightingale Lane | took Matthew the raven | Old Bailey's black feather came off a talking bird who works for a lady who dreams |
| 23.5 The way out | always; and if she died at the Wake | Lucienne shelves Neverwhere beside the Sandman ("room for two more"); Daniel is waiting to hear all of it |

Traits travel by themselves: her Sandman mercy is the same number in Neverwhere, so a merciful queen arrives already
merciful.

**Cost.** Six stills (the five openings and the shelf; Hob's still is the seventh) and one shared clip of Death; no
new character sheets (Lucienne, Daniel, Death and Hob were approved in the Sandman). About ¥7. The Sandman itself is not
edited: the bridge reads its save.

## 4. How it works in the game (for whoever builds the next bridge)

- Conditions can look into another book: `"sandman:FLAG"` (a flag that book set), `"sandman:@2.6"` (a scene she
  reached), `"sandman:last=end-A|end-B|end-C|end-R"` (of those scenes, the first listed is the one reached most
  recently). They work in beat `if`/`unless` and in choice and branch `requires`. Code: `flagOn` in
  `app/src/lib/state/profile.ts`.
- Each book keeps its own list of scenes reached (the Sandman's in `visited`, later books' in `visitedBy`), ordered
  by the latest visit, because scene ids repeat between books.
- `check-story` fails the build if a cross-book condition names a book, scene or flag that does not exist, so a
  renamed Sandman flag can never silently break Neverwhere. The simulator gives every play a random history in the
  other books, so every opening and echo is exercised; the tests check each Sandman ending picks its opening.
- Pictures used in many scenes take the prefix `nw-shared-` (Death's clip: `nw-shared-death-v`).

## 5. The next doors

**Neverwhere → Coraline (Gaiman).** Reason to go: a cat. In Gaiman's Sandman, cats walk between dreams, and the black
cat of *Coraline* can be the one who comes for her ◆. Openings: one per Neverwhere ending (the marquis's door, Door's
door, staying Below, the taxi). Thread: doors and keys: the Angel's key she carries from Neverwhere rattles in
Coraline's lock and does not fit (already in the series bible), teaching that relics are not shortcuts. Death keeps
the dead ends; Coraline's own rule stands: her face is never shown with button eyes or a needle. Echoes to draw from:
Door and the marquis (doors and debts), Anaesthesia (a lost child, like the ghosts), Hunter (courage while frightened).

**Coraline → The Alchemist (Coelho).** A different author, so the bridge stays in the frame. Reason to go: Santiago's
recurring dream of treasure at the Pyramids is one of the dreams in Lucienne's library, and it keeps ending before
the treasure. The Queen of Dreams knows what an unfinished dream is. Openings: one per Coraline ending. Thread: the
dream itself, and the two stones (Urim and Thummim) as the relic. Gaiman's characters stay out of the desert; Death
appears only at the dead ends, which sit outside the story; Lucienne speaks only in the library. Echoes: courage
(Coraline) becomes faith (Santiago); the button key catches the crystal merchant's eye (already in the series bible).

**The Fifth Door** (all four relics) stays as in `series-bible.md` §7, with one change: Death is there too, since she
has met Aurie at the edge of every book.
